<hgroup>

It was introduced in HTML5 (2009), deprecated in 2013, then resurrected in 2020 with a totally new meaning. Few HTML elements have such a checkered history. The modern <hgroup> groups a heading with a subheading or alternative title — and crucially, removes the secondary text from the document outline.

1 A Brief and Tumultuous History

Few elements have been on as wild a ride as <hgroup>. Understanding the history explains why so many tutorials (and developers) still get it wrong.

2009 Introduced in HTML5 (WD).

Original purpose: wrap a heading (e.g. <h1>) together with one or more of <h2>–<h6> used as a subheading, so that only the highest-ranked heading counted toward the document outline. This was tied to the proposed (but never-implemented) "outline algorithm".

2013 Removed from W3C spec.

The W3C dropped <hgroup> because the outline algorithm had never shipped in any browser, and authors were using <hgroup> to hide <h2>-ranked subheadings from accessibility tools — a clear anti-pattern.

2017–2019 WHATWG kept it alive.

While the W3C removed it, the WHATWG Living Standard retained a placeholder definition. Browsers never stopped parsing it; it just had no defined semantics.

April 2020 Re-introduced with NEW semantics.

Steve Faulkner proposed a new, accessibility-friendly definition: <hgroup> groups a single heading element with one or more <p> elements representing a subheading, tagline, or alternative title. The <p> contents are visible but do not create an extra outline entry.

2023+ Back in W3C HTML 5.3 drafts.

Now also part of the W3C HTML 5.3 working drafts. Finally safe to use — provided you use the modern syntax.

Common Mistake Older tutorials still teach the 2009 syntax: <hgroup><h1>Title</h1><h2>Subtitle</h2></hgroup>. This is wrong in modern HTML. Using <h2> inside <hgroup> is invalid; you must use <p> for the subheading.

2 The Modern Meaning (2020+)

The new <hgroup> is a wrapper that pairs a single heading element (<h1>–<h6>) with one or more <p> elements that act as a subtitle, tagline, or alternative title. The <p> content is fully visible but does not appear as a separate outline entry.

<hgroup>
  <h1>Rush</h1>
  <p>The unofficial history of the 1976 Formula 1 season</p>
</hgroup>

This solves a real, long-standing problem: how do you mark up a title plus a subtitle without the subtitle polluting the heading hierarchy? Before <hgroup>, authors had two bad choices:

Bad choice A — <h2>

Make the subtitle an <h2>. Problem: the subtitle is now a real section heading in the document outline, so screen-reader users get a spurious "section" jump.

Bad choice B — styled <span>

Style a <span> to look like a subtitle. Problem: semantics are lost — assistive tech has no idea of the relationship.

<hgroup> gives you the best of both worlds: the subtitle is semantically associated with the heading, and it stays out of the outline.

3 Syntax & Rules

The content model is strict. Violating it makes the markup invalid.

Child allowed inside <hgroup>How manyPurpose
A single heading element (<h1>–<h6>)Exactly one (required)The primary title
<p> elementsOne or more (optional)Subtitles, taglines, alternative titles

Valid patterns

<!-- Title + subtitle -->
<hgroup>
  <h1>Climate Report 2024</h1>
  <p>A year of records broken and lines crossed</p>
</hgroup>

<!-- Title + tagline -->
<hgroup>
  <h2>The New Yorker</h2>
  <p>Reporting since 1925</p>
</hgroup>

<!-- Heading alone — also valid (degenerate case) -->
<hgroup><h1>Just a title</h1></hgroup>

<!-- Title with multiple subtitles -->
<hgroup>
  <h1>Markdown</h1>
  <p>A lightweight markup language</p>
  <p>Or: how I learned to stop worrying and write prose</p>
</hgroup>

Invalid patterns

<!-- ❌ WRONG: using <h2> as a subheading -->
<hgroup>
  <h1>The Matrix</h1>
  <h2>Welcome to the real world</h2>
</hgroup>

<!-- ❌ WRONG: more than one heading -->
<hgroup>
  <h1>The Matrix</h1>
  <h1>Reloaded</h1>
</hgroup>

<!-- ❌ WRONG: div instead of p for subtitle -->
<hgroup>
  <h1>The Matrix</h1>
  <div>Welcome to the real world</div>
</hgroup>

<!-- ❌ WRONG: nested hgroup -->
<hgroup>
  <h1>The Matrix</h1>
  <hgroup>
    <h2>Reloaded</h2>
    <p>subtitle</p>
  </hgroup>
</hgroup>
Pro Tip The W3C Nu Html Checker (validator.w3.org/nu) correctly validates the modern <hgroup> syntax. If your markup passes, you're safe.

4 Effect on the Document Outline

Only the single heading inside <hgroup> contributes to the outline. The <p> children are skipped entirely — they are phrasing context, not headings.

Compare these two snippets. Both render visually similar, but their outlines differ dramatically:

<!-- Using hgroup: clean outline -->
<hgroup>
  <h1>Lord of the Flies</h1>
  <p>A novel by William Golding</p>
</hgroup>
<h2>Chapter 1: The Sound of the Shell</h2>
<p>...</p>
<h2>Chapter 2: Fire on the Mountain</h2>
<p>...</p>

Outline:

1. Lord of the Flies
  1.1. Chapter 1: The Sound of the Shell
  1.2. Chapter 2: Fire on the Mountain
<!-- Using <h2> as subtitle: polluted outline -->
<h1>Lord of the Flies</h1>
<h2>A novel by William Golding</h2>
<h2>Chapter 1: The Sound of the Shell</h2>
<p>...</p>
<h2>Chapter 2: Fire on the Mountain</h2>
<p>...</p>

Outline (note the bogus entry):

1. Lord of the Flies
  1.1. A novel by William Golding     ← spurious!
  1.2. Chapter 1: The Sound of the Shell
  1.3. Chapter 2: Fire on the Mountain
Common Mistake Tools like the "Headings Map" browser extension show the outline as parsed by the browser. Older extensions may not understand modern <hgroup> and will display the <p> as a heading. Use a current version, or check the spec instead.

6 Accessibility Considerations

The whole point of the 2020 redesign was to fix accessibility issues that plagued the original 2009 element.

How screen readers handle it

NVDA, JAWS, and VoiceOver treat <hgroup> as transparent: they announce the heading inside it normally, and the <p> is read as ordinary paragraph text in reading order. No phantom heading is created, and users navigate by the heading keyboard shortcut (e.g., H in NVDA) without picking up the subtitle.

Pro Tip For maximum screen-reader clarity, you can pair <hgroup> with aria-label on the heading itself to provide a shortened title (the in-page visible text stays the full version). However, this is rarely necessary — the default behaviour is already clean.

Why the old syntax hurt accessibility

The pre-2020 version allowed multiple heading levels inside <hgroup>, and the outline algorithm was supposed to mask the lower-ranked ones. But because no browser ever implemented the outline algorithm, screen readers saw every heading inside the <hgroup> as a real outline entry — so authors were effectively hiding content from sighted readers (visually) while exposing duplicate phantom headings to assistive tech. The 2020 redesign eliminates this entire class of bugs.

When to prefer a different pattern

SituationBetter than <hgroup>
Subtitle is a long-form deck paragraph (multiple sentences)Use a plain <p> after the <h1>, no wrapper
Subtitle is decorative / non-essentialPlain <span> styled with CSS
You need a separate section navigation entryUse a real <h2>, not <p>

7 Anti-Patterns to Avoid

Using <h2>–<h6> as the subheading

The single most common mistake. Inside <hgroup>, only a heading + <p> children are valid. Anything else is invalid HTML.

Using <span> or <div> for the subtitle

You lose the semantic relationship between the heading and its tagline. The whole point of <hgroup> is to mark that relationship.

Multiple headings in one <hgroup>

One heading, period. If you have two titles, you have two <hgroup>s (or two headings with no wrapper).

Wrapping every heading

Don't use <hgroup> for headings that have no subtitle. A bare <h1> is correct; wrapping it in <hgroup> just adds noise.

Styling <hgroup> with display:none

If the subtitle shouldn't appear, don't put it in markup. Hiding real content from everyone is bad UX and bad SEO.

Using <hgroup> inside a <button> or <a>

Phrasing content only — <hgroup> is flow content and can't go inside interactive phrasing elements.

8 Browser Support

Because <hgroup> has no DOM behaviour and no default styling beyond what its children provide, every browser has "supported" it for years — the only question is whether the modern content model is enforced.

Chrome 5+ Firefox 4+ Safari 5+ Edge 12+ Opera 11.5+ iOS Safari Android Chrome

Browsers do not enforce the content model — you can put anything inside <hgroup> and it will render. The W3C validator (validator.w3.org/nu) does enforce it, however, and that is your source of truth.

Default styling <hgroup> has no default UA styles — display:block and no margin, padding, font, or color. You'll want to add your own typography rules.

9 Try It Yourself

Exercise 1 — Convert legacy markup Take this legacy (2009-era) snippet and rewrite it using the modern <hgroup> syntax:

  <h1>Apollo 13</h1>
  <h2>Houston, we've had a problem</h2>
</hgroup>
Hint: only one element changes. Validate at validator.w3.org/nu when you're done.
Exercise 2 — Style a magazine masthead Build an <hgroup> for an online magazine where the main title uses a serif font at 3rem and the tagline uses a smaller italic serif. Add a thin rule beneath the group. (No JavaScript required.)
Exercise 3 — Inspect the outline Build a page with three sections, each with an <hgroup> containing an <h2> plus a <p> tagline. Then add subsections with <h3> inside each. Open a headings-map extension and confirm the taglines do NOT appear in the outline.

10 Real-World Use Cases

Blog / news mastheads

Site name + tagline at the top of every page. The most common real-world use of <hgroup>.

Movie, book, game databases

Title + tagline or logline on a detail page (IMDb, Goodreads, Steam).

Conference agendas

Talk title + catchphrase subtitle per session.

Marketing landing pages

Hero title + value proposition sub-line. (Often paired with a CTA button below.)

Documentation headers

API name + version tagline at the top of a docs page.

Product detail pages

Product name + marketing tagline above the price.
