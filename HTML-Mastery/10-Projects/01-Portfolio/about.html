Hi, I'm Maya.

I've spent the last nine years making the web a little more usable, one component, audit, and pull request at a time.

MC

Where I started

I grew up in a small town outside Eugene, Oregon, the kind of place where the public library was the internet. I taught myself HTML on a hand-me-down laptop to build a fan site for a local band, and that scrappy, learn-by-doing instinct never really left. I studied Human-Computer Interaction at the University of Washington because I wanted to understand not just how to build interfaces, but why some of them quietly fail the people who use them.

What I do now

Today I'm a senior frontend engineer at Northwind Labs, where I lead the design-systems guild and care a little too much about motion easing curves. Before Northwind, I shipped dashboards at a climate-tech startup, built accessibility tooling at a large e-commerce platform, and freelanced for nonprofits who needed a polished site on a tiny budget.

The thread connecting all of it is the same: care. Care for the person on the other side of the screen, for the engineer who'll inherit my code, and for the keyboard-only user who can't see the interface I'm so proud of. I think accessibility is the most under-celebrated form of craft in our industry, and I'd like to help change that.

How I work

I write small, well-named functions and favor composition over configuration. I default to semantic HTML before reaching for a div. I think the best pull requests are the boring ones, and that a Lighthouse score is a starting point, not a finish line. I mentor two junior engineers and run a monthly "frontend book club" where we read a chapter and argue about it over coffee.

Outside of work you'll find me trail-running in Forest Park, baking sourdough that's still only okay, and over-organizing my Notion workspace. I'm always happy to chat about accessibility, design systems, or whether the Cascade is really as straightforward as the spec implies.
