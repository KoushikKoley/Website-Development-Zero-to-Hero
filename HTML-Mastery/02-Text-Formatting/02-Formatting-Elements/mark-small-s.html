Section 2.2 · Formatting Elements

<mark> · <small> · <s> · <del> · <ins>

Five elements that mark up the edges of meaning: highlighted text, side comments, things no longer true, things removed, things added. Each carries a distinct semantic signal for users, search engines, and assistive tech.

1 Quick Comparison

ElementDefault lookSemantic meaningTypical use
<mark>Yellow backgroundHighlighted / relevant in contextSearch match, current section anchor
<small>Smaller fontSide comment, fine printDisclaimer, copyright, terms
<s>StrikethroughNo longer accurate / relevantSold-out items, corrected typos
<del>StrikethroughDeleted from a previous versionEdit tracking, diffs
<ins>UnderlineAdded in a new versionEdit tracking, diffs
Common Mistake <s> and <del> look identical (both strike through text) but mean different things. <s> = "this is no longer accurate." <del> = "this was deleted in an edit." Pick the right one for the right context.

2 <mark> — Highlight

The <mark> element represents text that is marked or highlighted for reference or notation due to its relevance in a particular context. Browsers render it with a yellow background by default, mimicking a highlighter pen.

Use case 1: Search results highlighting

<p>
  Your search for "<mark>coffee</mark>" returned 12 results.
  The <mark>coffee</mark> shop on 5th Avenue is open until 9 PM.
</p>

Your search for "coffee" returned 12 results. The coffee shop on 5th Avenue is open until 9 PM.

Use case 2: Quotation highlighting

When you quote a passage and want to draw attention to a specific part, wrap it in <mark>. This signals "the highlighter is mine, not in the original."

<blockquote>
  <p>
    The most important thing is to <mark>never stop questioning</mark>.
  </p>
  <cite>Albert Einstein</cite>
</blockquote>

Use case 3: Active section in a TOC

<nav>
  <a href="#intro">Introduction</a>
  <a href="#main"><mark>Main Content</mark></a>  <!-- current section -->
  <a href="#footer">Footer</a>
</nav>
Pro Tip <mark> is perfect for client-side search highlighting. Run a regex on text content and wrap matches in <mark> — the default yellow style is already perfect, or override with CSS for theme matching.

3 <small> — Side Comments

The <small> element represents side comments: small print, disclaimers, caveats, or copyright. Browsers render it one size smaller than surrounding text.

<p>
  Free shipping on orders over $50.
  <small>Offer expires December 31. See terms.</small>
</p>

<p>
  <small>© 2024 Acme Inc. All rights reserved.</small>
</p>

Free shipping on orders over $50. Offer expires December 31. See terms.

© 2024 Acme Inc. All rights reserved.

Info In HTML 4, <small> meant "smaller font". In HTML5 it was redefined as "side comments / fine print". The visual default is the same but the semantic meaning is now clear: use it for legal disclaimers, terms, copyright, attribution, and caveats — not just for shrinking any text.

Common uses

  • Copyright and attribution lines
  • Disclaimer text below product prices ("plus tax")
  • "Terms apply" notes
  • Last-updated timestamps in sidebars
  • Caveats after a fact or claim
Common Mistake Don't use <small> to make body text smaller for visual reasons. That's a job for CSS. <small> is for content that is genuinely a side comment.

4 <s> — No Longer Accurate

The <s> element represents text that is no longer accurate or no longer relevant. It renders as strikethrough. Use it when something has been corrected or superseded but you want to keep the original visible for context.

<p>
  The meeting is on <s>Monday</s> Tuesday at 3 PM.
</p>

<p>
  Price: <s>$99</s> $79 (limited time)
</p>

The meeting is on Monday Tuesday at 3 PM.

Price: $99 $79 (limited time)

Pro Tip For e-commerce sale prices, use <s>: "the original price is no longer the active price." For documents that are tracked for edits, use <del>: "this text was removed in revision X."

5 <del> — Deleted Text

The <del> element represents a removal from a document. It is intended for tracking edits — when you want to show that text was deleted in a revision, pair it with <ins> for the replacement.

<p>
  The committee voted to change the bylaws: membership fees
  will be <del>$25 per year</del> <ins>$30 per year</ins>
  effective January 1.
</p>

The committee voted to change the bylaws: membership fees will be $25 per year $30 per year effective January 1.

<del> supports two attributes for tracking edits:

  • cite — URL of a document explaining the change
  • datetime — Timestamp of the change (ISO 8601 format)
<p>
  <del cite="/changelog/v2.1" datetime="2024-03-15T10:00:00Z">
    The store is open 9-5 daily.
  </del>
  <ins cite="/changelog/v2.1" datetime="2024-03-15T10:00:00Z">
    The store is open 8-8 daily.
  </ins>
</p>

6 <ins> — Inserted Text

The <ins> element represents an addition to a document. Renders with an underline by default. Like <del>, it supports cite and datetime attributes.

<p>
  Please review the updated terms. <ins>New: accounts inactive for
  12 months will be archived.</ins>
</p>

Please review the updated terms. New: accounts inactive for 12 months will be archived.

Common Mistake Don't use <ins> for visual underlines on regular text — that's the job of CSS text-decoration: underline. <ins> carries semantic meaning: "this was added in an edit." Also, don't use <u> for underlines either, since users confuse it with links. Use CSS.

7 Pairing del & ins

The most common pattern is to wrap a deletion and an insertion together to show a "before/after" edit. This is exactly how wiki "diffs" and tracked-change views work.

<p>
  The deadline has been moved from
  <del>March 1</del> <ins>March 15</ins>.
</p>

<p>
  <del>All employees must wear suits.</del>
  <ins>Business casual attire is permitted.</ins>
</p>

Block-level edits

<del> and <ins> are unusual: they can wrap either inline text or block-level content. This makes them perfect for tracking whole-paragraph changes.

<del datetime="2024-04-01">
  <p>The product will launch in Q2 2024.</p>
</del>
<ins datetime="2024-04-01">
  <p>The product will launch in Q3 2024.</p>
</ins>
Pro Tip When tracking changes for legal or compliance reasons, always include the datetime attribute in ISO 8601 format. This creates a machine-readable audit trail.

8 Attributes: cite & datetime

Only <del> and <ins> support these attributes. They are ignored on <mark>, <small>, and <s>.

AttributeFormatPurposeExample
citeURLLink to changelog or source explaining the editcite="/changelog/v2"
datetimeISO 8601Timestamp of the changedatetime="2024-03-15T10:00Z"

ISO 8601 date formats

datetime="2024-03-15"                  <!-- date only -->
datetime="2024-03-15T10:00"             <!-- date + time, local -->
datetime="2024-03-15T10:00:00Z"        <!-- date + time, UTC -->
datetime="2024-03-15T10:00:00-05:00"   <!-- date + time, with offset -->

9 Real-World Examples

Search results with highlighting

<article>
  <h3>Result 1 of 12</h3>
  <p>
    The best <mark>coffee</mark> in town is at Brew & Co.
    Their <mark>coffee</mark> beans are roasted daily.
  </p>
  <small>Last updated: 2024-03-12</small>
</article>

Sale price display

<p class="price">
  <s>$99.99</s> <strong>$74.99</strong>
  <small>Sale ends Friday. Limit 2 per customer.</small>
</p>

Legal edit tracking

<p>
  <del datetime="2024-01-15T00:00:00Z" cite="/tos/v2-changes">
    We may share your data with partners.
  </del>
  <ins datetime="2024-01-15T00:00:00Z" cite="/tos/v2-changes">
    We will never sell your personal data.
  </ins>
</p>
<p><small>Effective January 15, 2024. <a href="/tos/v2-changes">View changelog</a>.</small></p>

Recipe correction

<p>
  Bake at <s>350°F</s> <ins>375°F</ins> for 25 minutes.
  <small>Updated 2024-02-01 after reader feedback.</small>
</p>

Article highlight with citation

<blockquote>
  <p>
    The single most important thing is to
    <mark>treat others as you would want to be treated</mark>.
  </p>
</blockquote>

10 Accessibility

  • <mark> — Most screen readers do not announce "highlighted" by default. If the highlight conveys meaning (e.g. a search match), consider adding aria-label or a visual indicator nearby.
  • <small> — No semantic announcement; purely visual size change.
  • <s> — Some screen readers announce "strikethrough" or read the text with reduced emphasis. Behavior varies.
  • <del> — Typically announced as "deleted" or "removed".
  • <ins> — Typically announced as "inserted" or "added".
Common Mistake If the visual styling alone conveys critical meaning (e.g. strike-through indicates "do not use this option"), add a text label too. Relying on strikethrough alone will fail for low-vision users.

12 Try It Yourself

Exercise 1 — Sale page Build a product card showing:
  • A product name
  • An original price struck through, and a sale price in bold
  • A small-print disclaimer about offer expiry
Exercise 2 — Changelog entry Write a one-paragraph "changelog" entry using <del> and <ins> with cite and datetime attributes. Change a date and a fee. Add a small "View full changelog" link.
Exercise 3 — Search highlighter Write a paragraph that mentions "coffee" three times. Wrap each occurrence in <mark>. Override the default yellow with a CSS class that gives the highlight a pink background. Verify it looks readable.
Exercise 4 — Pick the right element Match each scenario to one of mark, small, s, del, ins:
  • A "Last updated" timestamp in a sidebar
  • A corrected typo where the original should remain visible
  • A search-match highlight in search results
  • An inserted sentence in a legal revision with a date
  • A price drop shown on a product card

13 Summary

  • <mark> = relevant in current context (yellow highlight). Use for search matches and active TOC items.
  • <small> = side comment / fine print (smaller text). Use for disclaimers, copyright, caveats.
  • <s> = no longer accurate (strikethrough). Use for corrected facts and sale prices.
  • <del> = removed in an edit (strikethrough). Supports cite and datetime.
  • <ins> = added in an edit (underline). Supports cite and datetime.
  • Always include datetime in ISO 8601 format on tracked edits.
  • <del> and <ins> can wrap block content as well as inline text.
